# Market Day: district commerce

The old unlimited supply/commission cycle produced five points and net coins without property or location. New rooms use `rulesVersion: 2`; unversioned rooms retain their original economy, movement, scoring, turn order and unlimited commissions. Constructor's third argument selects version 1 for fixtures. No database migration is required.

The revised competitive objective is highest prosperity after exactly ten turns per player. Equal final scores share victory. Each round rotates the starting seat; no player can close the game early. Each turn remains move → one business action → optional trade. Income and the visible forecast settle after the last turn, including the final round.

Three public contracts remain available for two rounds, then refresh in rounds 3/5/7/9. Each district has one claim; serving it removes it for rivals. A player must visit the district and own a property there. Later major orders require two properties or an upgraded property. Local orders cost two supplies and two coins; major orders cost three of each. Rewards are 5/6/7 or 8/9/10 points across Sunrise/Palm/Lantern, compensating for higher acquisition prices. There is no coin payout. Two-round availability provides time to acquire property and serve it without adding free actions.

Wholesale supplies cost four coins per pair, from shared stock of two times (player count minus one), replenished each round. In a legal round, earlier buyers can exhaust stock before the final player acts; rotated initiative moves that access advantage each round. Upgrades consume a supply as well as four coins. Base income is two plus property levels capped at four; savings points remain capped at ten. Property, contract, and stock decisions compete for ten business actions. Buying wholesale and selling the same pair yields no cash advantage and costs two turns.

District sets score 3/7/12 bonus points for 2/3/4 properties, without income multiplication. A mixed portfolio may be profitably swapped: a Sunrise-for-Palm exchange can complete both players' sets. Both parties see projected set changes and remaining coins/supplies before sending an offer. Accepted/declined trades end the active turn; unanswered offers remain cancellable.

## Evidence

`tests/market-redesign.test.ts` verifies ownership/location/resource/actor gating, contested single-use orders, atomic failures, shared-stock depletion and refill, upgrade costs, reciprocal set-completing trades, unversioned economics, contract seasons, exact equal turns for 2/3/4 players, rotating starting seats, terminal command rejection and shared ties.

A deterministic tournament runs 192 four-player matches across 48 seeds and all four policy/seat rotations. Mean prosperity: investment 40.33, order-focused 38.02, district-focused 42.63, old restock/commission spam 4.50. Wins including ties: investment 56, orders 28, district sets 124, spam zero. Seat wins including ties: 56/52/44/56. An earlier route rule depended on seat index and produced a 68/64/32/64 distribution; removing the seat offset for version 2 reduced that avoidable bias. Another 288 two-player matches compare all three viable policies across 48 seeds in both seat assignments. Order/investment wins: 52/36 with 8 ties; district/investment: 76/16 with 4 ties; district/order: 60/36 with no ties. Both policies in every pairing have winning seeds. Policy comparisons are sensitive to heuristics; this run supports keeping contract rewards unchanged pending human playtesting. The legal scarcity test runs full 2/3/4-player rounds without injected stock: the first N−1 buyers deplete supply, the last is rejected atomically, then stock returns with the next starting player. These are bounded heuristic comparisons, not proof of perfect human balance. District acquisition is intentionally important; negotiation is not represented in automated policies.

## Presentation and motion

A stable clockwise 12-property perimeter encloses the central exchange with stock and closing forecast. Original SVG buildings show level pips; ownership uses a player-color border and trader location uses numbered tokens. Mobile retains the geographic ring with readable labels. The goal, personal rival gap, active trader and turn phase are explicit. Public contracts show costs, prerequisites, depletion and disabled reasons. Final outcome shows rank, winners, all scoring components and the winner's largest contribution.

Authoritative movement animates fixed-size numbered trader tokens in a shared overlay through perimeter waypoints in 350–1100ms using the Web Animations API. No canvas, perpetual rendering loop or uncommitted optimistic move is involved. Identical polled snapshots do not restart motion; new authoritative movement cancels the previous effect and settles to the current position. Reduced motion and hidden-tab movement skip travel. Finite 300–350ms building/score settle effects identify confirmed ownership, level and score changes. Author-run visual simulation covered 320px, 390px and 1440px viewports, move/business/end-turn actions, and a final-result fixture. It revealed and fixed nonuniform SVG token scaling: traders now remain circular at 24px while translating along measured board waypoints. Evidence under `qa-redesign/author-market` explicitly excludes HTTP/database claims. Independent room verification remains separate.
